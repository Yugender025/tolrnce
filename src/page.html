From a drawing to a solid

Open a DWG or DXF, a photo of a drawing, or describe the part in words. TolRnce builds a real solid on the OpenCASCADE geometry kernel, with parameters you can change and features you can switch off.

Reads
DWG, DXF, photos of drawings, STEP AP203, AP214 and AP242
Builds
Extrudes, cuts, holes with ISO fits, revolves, fillets, chamfers, shells, patterns and bent tube

It reads a part the way you do

Drop in a supplier’s STEP file and a trained feature network labels every face: through holes, blind holes, counterbores, countersinks, slots, pockets, steps, bosses, chamfers and fillets. Each feature is checked against the exact geometry, and sizes always come from the geometry.

It also finds tube sockets, NPT ports and mounting holes, so a vendor part drops straight into an assembly.

Every hole knows its fit

Tolerances are part of the model: ISO 286 fits on holes and shafts, ISO 2768 general tolerances on the drawing, and a stack-up calculator for chains of dimensions. Try a fit on a ⌀20 bore.

⌀19.985 mm

Slides in by hand. Clearance 0.015 to 0.036 mm. In the H7/g6 sliding fit range.

Bore ⌀20 H7: 20.000 to 20.021 mm. The gap is drawn 100 times larger.

Assemblies that bolt together

Place parts with mates, then bolt a set of holes. Screw lengths come from the joint stack, every hole is checked for alignment, and clashes are caught before anything is made.

The bill of materials and the cost roll-up update as you go, with ISO screws, nuts, washers, dowels and bearings from the built-in library.

Panels, skids and tubing

Fittings, valves, gauges and manifolds come with ports. Connect them, and TolRnce routes tube around every part on the panel, places cushion clamps where tube crosses the strut, and writes the cut list.

The same works for vendor parts: their ports are found when you open the STEP file.

Into the CAD program you already use

Every part and assembly exports as a package of ordinary files. Open the STEP anywhere, or run a macro and get the full feature tree in your own CAD program, ready to edit.

Your drawings stay with you

Design files are your company’s IP. TolRnce is built so they don’t have to leave your computer.

Geometry runs on your machine
The CAD kernel runs inside your browser or the desktop app. Drawings and STEP files you open are read there; there’s no TolRnce server they get uploaded to.
The desktop app works offline
It makes no network requests, so you can use it on computers without internet access.
You decide what the AI sees
The AI agent in the web studio sends your request and the current model to Claude, made by Anthropic, only when you ask it to do something.
No lock-in
Every part exports as STEP, and the macros rebuild it as native features in your own CAD program, where you own it.
